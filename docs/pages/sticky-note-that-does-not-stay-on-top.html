A desktop sticky note that doesn't stay on top

Every sticky note app makes the same bet: it is always on top, so you can never lose sight of your notes. That bet is wrong about half the time.

The two options you actually get

Open Windows and look for a way to keep a note on your wallpaper. You get two designs, and both of them are a compromise:

  • Always on top. The note floats above every window. Useful for thirty seconds, then it covers the thing you are working on. You drag it to a corner, it covers something else.
  • In the tray. The note is behind an icon. You will not open it. Things you have to click to see stop existing.

The first design fails because it fights the window manager. The second fails because it asks you to remember it exists.

The third option: be a normal window

A window does not need a title bar to participate in z-order. If you remove the chrome and do not set the topmost flag, you get something that behaves like every other window on your desktop: when you click your editor, the card goes behind it. When you click the card, it comes forward.

That is the whole idea. It is not a trick, and it is not "always at the bottom" either — it is simply a window that never asks for special treatment:

What it feels like: the card sits on your wallpaper like an icon that happens to have text rows on it. It gets covered. It comes back when you reach for it. You stop noticing it, which is the point.

On Windows this means three things: overrideredirect(True) so Tk stops drawing a title bar, no WS_EX_TOPMOST so Windows does not pin it above everything, and a rounded window region so the rectangle does not look like a dialog box. The full technique — including the corners and the translucency — is written up in the Tkinter page.

What this costs you, honestly

A frameless window gives up more than a title bar:

  • No taskbar entry. There is no button to click, so you need another way to find the card — in Plan Card it stays where you left it.
  • No Alt+Tab entry. The same reason. If it gets fully buried under a maximised window, you have to minimise that window to see it again.
  • You drag it yourself. There is no snap-to-edge behaviour unless you write it.
  • Nothing is on top when you need it most. If you want the card to cover your browser, it will not, and that is deliberate.

If any of those are deal-breakers, a normal always-on-top widget is a better fit and you should use one. This is a specific trade, not a universal improvement.

If you want the other trade instead
